# Balance — story and factions

Working notes from the designer. Everything here is provisional: faction names, insignia,
units and detailed mechanics are still open, and the colour names are placeholder
identifiers. The setting's peoples and religions are fictional.

## Factions

### Blue — freedom, justice, self-determination
A democratic coalition against arbitrary power, and generally the sympathetic side.
- **Flaws:** bureaucracy, slow decisions, arrogance, losing touch with ordinary citizens.
- **Themes:** protection, restoration, shared morale.
- **Suggested gameplay strengths:** keeping scarce units alive and coordinating them;
  healing and repair. Not yet balanced or final.

### Red — order, power, centralized command
Authoritarian, ruthless and decisive. Red promises peace through unified strength and
believes the ends can justify the means. Not cartoon villains: its leadership and
logistical effectiveness have real appeal.
- **Suggested gameplay strengths:** offensive power, coordinated strikes, swift action.
  Costs and weaknesses TBD.

### Green — opportunism: merchants and local lords
A loose collection of self-interested actors with no interest in ruling the galaxy. They
ally with either side when it pays. Their story role matters; don't dismiss it.
- **Suggested style:** ambush, stealth, lethal equipment; weak cohesion as a large army.

### Palatinate / Clergy — an ancient covert religious order
Antagonistic or unpredictable. Sometimes they oppose every faction, sometimes they quietly
back one.
- **History:** they once spread religions among intelligent civilizations to impose social
  order. The plan was discredited when it set off sectarian conflicts nobody intended.
- **Now:** a new guard has replaced the old one. It prefers hidden guidance and
  manipulation, in the manner of a covert intellectual order.
- **Powers and technology:** not yet defined.

## Sides and campaigns (how the game is structured)
- **Blue and Red are the main playable factions**, each with a full classic campaign. Both
  campaigns start with the same first mission, **First Claim**, seen from either side:
  Blue's version is on a tropical forest planet, Red's on a desert planet (planets not
  named yet).
- **Green and the Palatinate** get mini-campaigns or single missions later.
- The player plays **one faction per map**. The others are played by the AI.
- **Alliances are set per map**: on a given map an AI faction (Green, the Palatinate, or
  Blue/Red) may fight alongside either side, or against everyone. Example: in the Ashfall
  skirmish, Green's merchants side with the player's opponent.
- **Orichalcum** is the crystal that funds armies; whoever holds a field can pay for ships,
  arms and soldiers. In First Claim the only field on the world is the objective.

## Political backstory
A just and widely respected king, called **the Peacebringer**, united most of his home
planet through diplomacy and brought the neighbouring planets of the system into an
alliance. Peace and prosperity followed.

Believing large ground forces were no longer needed, he abruptly discharged most of the
military. He kept the Royal Guard and the police and relied heavily on automated orbital
defences. Many discharged military families and the workers around them suffered
economically and lost social standing. Others saw the policy as a strategic danger.

A young lieutenant used the backlash to organize a military coup during a palace
ceremony. He slaughtered the king and seized power. The king's only son, aged 17, arrived
late to the ceremony and escaped with loyalists. A brave woman of the Royal Guard helped
him get away. Over the following years they had an on-and-off romance, but they have not
been together for some time.

**The game begins ten years later:**

| Character | Age | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Former prince | 27 | Future Blue leader |
| Coup leader | 40 | Red leader |
| Royal Guard veteran | ~37 | Blue field commander, the Blue leader's right hand |

The field commander is more than a romantic supporting character: she needs her own goals
and her own judgment.

**Open lore question:** how did a coup led by a lieutenant succeed despite the automated
orbital defences and the king's surviving loyalists? Don't commit to an answer yet.
